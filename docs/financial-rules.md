# Financial controls in Touchline

Reference date: **4 October 2026**. Initial rule baseline: **2026/27**. These controls apply to both the managed club and AI clubs. The figures in a career are simulated accounts, not an assessment of any real club's compliance.

## What the simulation measures

- Opening club revenue and registration book values are estimates. They are frozen as the career's starting assumptions; increasing wages or adding cash through the Editor does not automatically increase permitted spending.
- A transfer consumes cash immediately, including signing and agent fees. Its registration cost is also allocated across the agreed contract length, capped at five years, for annual squad-cost control. This allocation is not an instalment-payment option.
- Annual wages, projected bonuses and applicable coaching costs count towards squad expenditure. Portuguese and EFL wages policies use their own numerators; the interface identifies the active constraint. The Championship income basis includes net transfer cash flow, while ordinary-revenue wages checks exclude sale profits.
- A sale creates a profit or loss relative to the player's remaining simulated book value. Gross sale proceeds are not all treated as profit. A player's estimated market value alone does not become club revenue.
- Gate receipts use the match's saved attendance and simulated ticket price. Income is posted once per match, including AI home matches; neutral-venue receipts are shared equally. This is a game convention, not a general competition regulation.
- Weekly income is based on the club's fixed forecast. Weekly wages include staff. Each buyer must also retain the game's cash reserve.
- Every applicable policy is checked at signing, for user and AI transfers alike. AI clubs select players to address squad needs and must also satisfy cash, transfer-budget and wage-budget checks. World transfer history records completed transactions.

The simulation does **not** restrict all clubs to a fixed number of signings. It restricts unaffordable commitments. It does not implement a full auditing system, regulatory appeals, overdue-tax schedules, debt financing, fines, points deductions or transfer embargo adjudication. Existing careers begin financial tracking on migration; the game does not fabricate audited results for their earlier seasons. Published real-world rule changes require a future code update rather than appearing automatically in later simulated years.
